# 12. Dual-Magnet Levitation — Multibody Modeling

The single-magnet system of [§1](01_system_modeling.md)–[§11](11_comparative_analysis.md) is a SISO plant with attractive-only force. The dual-magnet configuration introduces a vertical sandwich of two electromagnets acting on a common mobile sled, providing **bidirectional force capability**. The control problem in this part of the project is intentionally kept simple — a single state-space controller is implemented — because the **central goal here is to validate the Simscape Multibody modeling workflow** that will scale to the sled-unit model in [§13](13_sled_unit_levitation.md).

## 12.1 Configuration and Dynamics

Two electromagnets are arranged symmetrically above and below the mobile sled, each paired with its own ferromagnetic armature. The upper magnet pulls the sled upward, the lower magnet pulls it downward, and gravity acts downward. The 1-D vertical equilibrium condition is
$$F_{m,\text{up}} = F_{m,\text{lw}} + F_g$$
There is no rotational moment to consider. The controller drives the sled to a state in which the two air gaps are equal by default, but the design script accepts independent target values for the upper and lower gaps if asymmetric operation is desired.

The z-axis of each magnet surface points outward (into the air gap); the z-axis of each armature surface points inward (into the armature body).

The dynamics is one-dimensional, but the modeling apparatus introduced here scales without modification to 6-DOF systems — which is the entire point of the exercise.

## 12.2 Why Multibody Instead of Hand-Derived Equations

For the single-magnet plant in [§1.1](01_system_modeling.md), writing $m\ddot{x} = K_M\,i_s^2/x_s^2 - mg$ by hand was straightforward. As soon as the system gains additional bodies, joints, or degrees of freedom, hand-derivation becomes error-prone: every body needs a kinematic transformation, every force needs projection to the center of mass, every constraint needs explicit handling.

Simscape Multibody removes that burden. The user defines bodies with geometry, mass, and frames; connects them with joints; and applies forces at their natural points of action. The solver computes the equivalent dynamics automatically. This chapter exists to **build confidence in that workflow on a problem simple enough to verify analytically**, before applying the same workflow to a system whose closed-form equations would be impractical.

## 12.3 Frame Convention

Multibody models live or die by frame discipline. The convention used consistently throughout this folder is:

- **The z-axis of every magnet surface frame points outward from the magnet** (away from the magnet body, into the air gap).
- **The z-axis of every armature surface frame points inward into the armature** (away from the air gap, into the armature material).

The two frames in each magnet–armature pair therefore have collinear z-axes pointing in the same physical direction across the gap. With this convention:

- The Reluctance Force Actuator's R (reference) and C (case) ports can be assigned identically across all magnet–armature pairs — no sign-flipping case-by-case.
- The force normal vector is encoded implicitly in the frame orientation.
- The Transform Sensor (`PositionSensor`) that measures each air gap is connected between the magnet surface and the armature surface with the same B/F port assignment for both upper and lower pairs, and the measurement sign comes out consistent.

Getting frame conventions wrong is the single most common source of bugs in multibody magnetic models; getting them right makes the rest of the model self-consistent.

## 12.4 Key Blocks

- **Reluctance Force Actuator** — generates the magnetic force from current and gap inputs. The R and C ports define the magnet–armature pair between which the force acts.
- **Translational Multibody Interface** — bridges the magnetic and mechanical domains. Force flows from the magnetic side to the mechanical side; velocity flows back from the mechanical side, keeping the kinematic state of the two domains consistent.
- **Prismatic Joint** (or Cartesian Joint for higher-DOF generalization) — defines the allowed translational motion of the sled relative to ground.
- **Transform Sensor** (`PositionSensor`) — measures the relative position between two frames; here, the air gap of each magnet–armature pair.
- **External Force and Torque** — injects disturbances at any frame, used to test rejection performance.

These blocks compose into a model whose equations are generated by the Simscape solver, not written by the user — which is exactly the point of the exercise.

## 12.5 Two Implementation Variants

The folder contains two models that produce equivalent dynamics via different modeling philosophies:

- **`Maglev_Dual_ResultantForce.slx`** — the two magnetic forces are computed externally, summed analytically into a single resultant, and applied at the sled's center of mass through a single Multibody Interface. Explicit and easy to inspect for a 1-D problem, but the modeler is responsible for performing the force projection by hand.
- **`Maglev_Dual_LocalForce.slx`** — each magnetic force is applied at its own local frame (the magnet surface). The multibody framework automatically converts these locally-applied forces into motion at the sled center of mass, using the frame geometry. Identical results for this 1-D case, but the only viable approach for general 3-D, multi-DOF systems.

The 1-D dual-magnet problem is a good testbed precisely because the two approaches can be cross-validated: any disagreement between the resultant-force and local-force trajectories indicates a frame or sign error in the local-force formulation. Once they agree, the local-force methodology can be lifted to the sled-unit model with confidence.

## 12.6 Controller Design

The controller is a state-space controller with integral action — the same design pattern as [§3](03_state_space_control.md), redone for the linearized dual-magnet plant. The design script `design_state_space_control_dualMaglev.m` proceeds in three steps.

**Step 1 — Equilibrium currents via constrained optimization.** The force-balance equation
$$K_m\,\frac{i_{\text{up}}^2}{x_{\text{up},0}^2} \;-\; K_m\,\frac{i_{\text{lw}}^2}{x_{\text{lw},0}^2} \;=\; m\,g$$
admits a one-parameter family of solutions in the $(i_{\text{up}}, i_{\text{lw}})$ plane. The selected pair $(i_{\text{up},0}, i_{\text{lw},0})$ is the one that minimizes total electrical power $i_{\text{up}}^2 + i_{\text{lw}}^2$ subject to the equilibrium constraint and the amplifier current bounds, solved numerically with `fmincon`.

**Step 2 — Differential drive and linearization.** The two physical currents are parameterized by a single scalar deviation:
$$i_{\text{up}} = i_{\text{up},0} + i_{\text{dev}},\qquad i_{\text{lw}} = i_{\text{lw},0} - i_{\text{dev}}$$
A positive $i_{\text{dev}}$ produces a net upward force by simultaneously increasing the upper current and decreasing the lower current. This **collapses the two physical inputs into a single control variable**, making the plant SISO and matching the structure of [§3](03_state_space_control.md) exactly.

The sled is rigid, so a position deviation $x$ (positive upward) makes the upper gap smaller by $x$ and the lower gap larger by $x$. Substituting and taking the first-order Taylor expansion yields
$$m\,\ddot{x} = k_x\,x + k_i\,i_{\text{dev}}$$
with the dual-magnet stiffness coefficients
$$k_x = 2 K_m\!\left(\frac{i_{\text{up},0}^2}{x_{\text{up},0}^3} + \frac{i_{\text{lw},0}^2}{x_{\text{lw},0}^3}\right),\qquad k_i = 2 K_m\!\left(\frac{i_{\text{up},0}}{x_{\text{up},0}^2} + \frac{i_{\text{lw},0}}{x_{\text{lw},0}^2}\right)$$

Both coefficients are strictly positive — each magnet pair contributes additively, so the open-loop plant remains unstable, exactly as in [§1.4](01_system_modeling.md). **Sanity check**: setting $i_{\text{lw},0} = 0$ reduces these expressions to $k_x = 2mg/x_{\text{up},0}$ and $k_i = 2mg/i_{\text{up},0}$, recovering the single-magnet linearization identically.

**Step 3 — Augmented state-space controller.** Following [§3](03_state_space_control.md), the integral of position is added as a state:
$$\mathbf{z} = [x, v, q]^\top,\qquad \dot{\mathbf{z}} = A_{\text{aug}}\,\mathbf{z} + B_{\text{aug}}\,i_{\text{dev}}$$
The feedback gain $K$ is computed by pole placement or LQR exactly as in [§3](03_state_space_control.md). The controller outputs the virtual input $i_{\text{dev}}$, which is mapped back to physical current commands $(i_{\text{up}}, i_{\text{lw}})$ via the differential-drive relation of Step 2.

**Observer.** The controller uses the ideal velocity signal directly — no EKF. The focus of this chapter is the multibody modeling workflow, not observer design. Adding an EKF would be a direct extension of [§8](08_ekf.md).

The gain matrix, the equilibrium currents, and the stiffness coefficients are saved to `Controller_Params/DualMagnetControllerData.mat`. Simulation parameters and the multibody geometry are configured in `setup_dualMaglev_system.m`, structured analogously to `setup_sim_params.m` of folder `02`.
